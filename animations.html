Data input

2020-2026, ground reference: 213 passes, 8 Jan 2020 to 24 Aug 2026. TerraSAR-X, the newer processing. Every movement is measured against the ground points the two processings share, the reference the team adopted on 5 October 2026, so a rate here means relative to the street, not an absolute movement.

The switch also sets the 3D view. Either way every colour and every dot is a mean over at least 20 radar points, shown with its spread; how the ground reference works is on the Reference page, and what can and cannot yet be read from these signals is on the Estimation page.

Six players, drawn in the browser from small tables of averages. Each moves through the 213 passes of the chosen data set, or through one year; the line under each one says which pass you are looking at and what the weather was.

Play, pause and step with the buttons, drag the slider to scrub, or focus a player and use the arrow keys and the space bar. The players pause when scrolled out of view.

The scene, pass by pass

Kop van Zuid on a 10 m grid, north up; the ground-only grid stays at 25 m because those points are three times sparser. Each cell is coloured by how much its points moved since the previous pass, on a scale that never changes. On most passes the whole pier turns one shade together: that common change is the atmosphere over the whole scene, the reference point, or both.Measured against the ground, the median of the reference ground points is zero on every pass by construction, so a shade shared by the whole pier largely drops out and what is left is how buildings and quays move relative to the street. The height-dependent part we are after is the small extra colour on the tall buildings, which is why the trace of s(t) runs underneath.

The height gradient, pass by pass

Above, the mean displacement of ten height bands against their height, relative to the scene mean, with the fitted line for that pass; its slope is s(t). Below, the series of that slope over the whole record, with a marker following the frame. The tilt above and the curve below are the same quantity, and most of the tilt is seasonal and slow: in summer the high bands sit to the right, in winter to the left.

Taking the gradient apart

The same s(t) in four steps: as measured; the slow trend removed, the north-east towers sinking faster than the ground, which in this set is small (-0.03 mm per 100 m per year); the yearly wave removed, the buildings expanding in summer; and the remainder differenced from pass to pass. That leftover, about 1.6 mm per 100 m, is 7 times the formal noise of the fit and about 15 N-units if it were all air. It is a candidate, not a proof.That does not make it air: the estimation pilot shows that the fit for the buildings can absorb air that follows the temperature.

Buildings or air?

Left, the 11-day changes of s(t), of a lagged air temperature standing in for the buildings, and of the lowest 100 m of refractivity above De Bilt. Right, the change of s(t) against each of the two, one dot per pass filling in as time advances, with the fitted line and, dashed, what physics would give if that explanation carried all of it. In the September reading both fitted lines are real and both fall short by the same third; Both fitted lines are real: the temperature line reaches 72 % of what the physics would give and the refractivity line 42 %. The year clock

One year on a clock face, January at the top. As the hand sweeps, each pass drops a dot: the open ground in blue, the buildings above 40 m in red, after offset, trend and curvature are removed per point. The lines are the monthly means; the dotted trace inside is the air temperature, monthly means over 2014-20232014-2026. The buildings peak in late July with the air. The open ground peaks in February, half a year away and later than a mirror image would.Measured against the ground, the open ground hardly moves through the year (0.13 mm), as expected when the reference is itself made of ground points. The buildings above 40 m swing 2.9 mm and peak around day 217, a little after the air (day 205). The ground at the towers' feet (tick the box) swings 0.53 mm and peaks around day 229, closer in time to the buildings than to the open ground.

A virtual balloon

For every pair of consecutive passes a balloon rises from the lowest height bin to 89 m, the highest radar point of the set, and drops a dot at each bin: how much that bin's residual changed over the 11 days, relative to the lowest bin. The red line is the fit through the dots and its slope is the layer gradient, the one number per pass the project retrieves today. The dashed line is what the De Bilt sounding predicts for the same 11 days. On most passes the dots fall close to a straight line, and the two series on the right follow each other with a correlation of about 0.47.
