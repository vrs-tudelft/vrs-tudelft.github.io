About the data set

Why does no radar point sit above 83 m? openIt decides whether the method's ceiling in this city is 83 m or 150 m, and how the towers' upper halves could be used.Roof heights from the 3D building register reach 158 m inside the scene; every tower above 83 m stops at 57 to 69 m of radar points (figure 30). Which height datum do the point heights use? partlyEverything called "above ground" rests on it.The ground model minus the geoid gives 3.0 to 3.5 m above sea level for every point, tower or pavement, so it is a terrain model and heights above it are safe.Confirmation from the producer.
What is the sign convention of the displacements? openWhether "positive" is towards or away from the satellite decides the sign of every rate and of the retrieved refractivity change.The site reads positive as towards the satellite; sinking towers then have negative rates, which is physically sensible. The seasonal phases (buildings peak in summer) fit the same reading.Confirmation from the producer; the physics would also be self-consistent the other way round.
Where is the reference point, how high, and on what does it stand? openAll displacements are relative to it. If it sits on a structure that expands in summer, every point inherits the mirror image; its height sets how much near-surface refractivity cycle the ground points see.The open ground peaks on day 45, a pure mirror of the buildings would peak on day 28, the towers' feet show nothing; the scene mean drifts, so no per-pass mean was removed.The producer's record of the reference point.
Is 10 January 2014 the master image of the processing as well as the zero of the series? partlyThe atmospheric state of the master enters every pass; whether it was a winter or a summer day matters for the stratification signal.All 20 594 series are exactly zero on that date.The processing record.
What is the local hour of the pass, and does the ascending track exist for this scene? openThe hour decides which weather data can be compared, and whether the night-time heat island is still there. A second track would flip the horizontal terms and separate several of the puzzles below.From the dawn-dusk orbit the descending pass should be around 06:40 solar time and the ascending one around 17:20; the file carries dates only.The acquisition metadata.
What does the quality column mean? openIt may carry information the coherence does not.Mapped in figure 01; not used elsewhere.Its definition from the producer.
Was an atmospheric filter applied in the processing? openA standard step removes a smooth atmospheric phase screen from every point. If it was applied, part of our signal may already be gone, which could be why both fitted coefficients are 60 to 70 % of prediction.Nothing in the file says.The processing record.

Can the high-resolution De Bilt soundings be obtained? openThe public archive has about one level in our layer; only the original files, with a reading every few metres, can validate a profile shape rather than a mean gradient.The archive's first level above the ground is 650 m up in the median; only 1 % of soundings have any level inside 0 to 100 m. The raw files are known to exist with about 3 500 records per ascent.Access to the KNMI raw sounding files.
Is distance or time of day the gap between the balloon and the radar? answeredDecides whether a closer station or a different hour would help more.On the 49 pass days with both a midnight and a noon sounding, the noon one explains the radar no better (figure 16). Time of day is not the gap; distance probably is.A sounding or tower profile over the city itself.
What does a weather model say over Rotterdam at the pass hour? openThe Dutch forecast model at 2.5 km would give a profile at the right place and hour, the balloon gives neither.Not yet requested; the pass hour is needed first.Model output for the 288 pass times.

About the physics

QuestionWhy it mattersWhat we checkedWhat would settle it
Why are both fitted coefficients 60 to 70 % of what physics predicts? openEither the buildings are not free to expand and the balloon is not representative, or part of the signal was removed in processing, or the model is incomplete.Temperature 0.62 of 0.92, refractivity 0.064 of 0.109, consistently across the whole scene and the south-west half. The sideways-expansion test gives 70 % of free expansion for the buildings, which fits the first reading for the temperature term.The atmospheric-filter question above, and a sounding over the city.
Why does the open ground move in winter? openA 0.5 to 0.8 mm yearly term at the ground is the size of the stratification signal; the lowest bin is not a clean zero.Three candidates (reference point, near-surface refractivity cycle, real motion of the fill); the spatial pattern argues against a simple mirror image. Details.The reference point's location and height; the ascending track.
What is the kink at 20 to 30 m in the binned profile? openIf it is air, the retrieval can be a profile; if it is the buildings, it is a nuisance to model.A line explains 65 % of the binned variance and a parabola 78 %; the departure has a fixed shape on every pass. Building podiums and setbacks cluster at those heights.A second track, or a per-building model that removes the structure first.
Which temperature does a building feel? partlyThe expansion term depends on the temperature of the structure, not of the air.A five-day lag of the daily mean air temperature fits best; the buildings follow about half to two thirds of the air's yearly swing (4 K against 7 K).A building with a known thermal history, or wall temperatures from a thermal satellite over the same blocks.
Do the newest towers shrink or settle? partlyThe tilt (rate against height within a building) enters the height gradient directly.Tilt −0.7 to −4.9 mm per 100 m per year, largest for the youngest concrete; a height error leaking into velocity in the processing is the alternative. Details.The producer's estimate of the height-velocity coupling for this stack.

About usefulness

Three questions we would put to a boundary-layer meteorologist, and have not yet:

  1. Which questions about temperature and moisture in the lowest hundred metres above a city matter most to you, and which of them does a street-level measurement fail to answer?
  2. What height range, vertical spacing, repeat frequency and accuracy would a profile need to be useful: is one layer mean every 11 days for ten years, at one fixed hour, worth anything, or is the profile shape indispensable?
  3. What independent observation would you want next to a layer-average refractivity change to trust it as temperature or humidity?

The same three questions, answered by a forecaster, would tell us whether the product is an evaluation data set for urban boundary-layer schemes or a new observation type for assimilation. Nobody has asked a paying customer yet either.
