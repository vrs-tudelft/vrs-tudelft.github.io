Kop van Zuid

The test set covers the Wilhelminapier and its surroundings on the south bank of the Nieuwe Maas in Rotterdam: about 530 by 450 m of quays, a few old warehouses, and a row of towers built between 2000 and 2017, among them De Rotterdam (149 m) and the Maastoren (165 m). It was chosen because it has high-rise. It is a best case for the method, not a city average, and the plots say so wherever it matters.

288 passes over ten years

TerraSAR-X passed over the scene 288 times between 10 January 2014 and 30 December 2023, on a descending (southbound) track, looking to the west-north-west at 23° from vertical. The nominal interval is 11 days; 89 % of the steps are exactly that, the largest gap is 44 days, and every season is covered about equally. The hour of the pass is not in the file. From the orbit it should be around 06:40 in the morning, but that is our arithmetic, not a confirmed value.

The points

The file holds 20 594 persistent scatterers: reflectors that returned a stable echo on every pass. For each it gives the position, several heights, a coherence (how well the echo fits a stable-point model, 0 to 1), a quality number whose definition we do not have, the fitted linear rate, quadratic term and yearly wave of its motion, and the 288 displacements themselves, in metres along the line of sight, all zero on the first pass. Most of the analysis uses the 11 818 points with coherence above 0.6.

The 83 m ceiling open

No radar point in the set is higher than 83.2 m above ground, although De Rotterdam reaches 149 m and the Maastoren 165 m, and the 3DBAG confirms four buildings above 100 m inside the scene. Three candidate causes, none confirmed:

  • A processing window. The point heights may have been searched only up to a limit. The processor can tell.
  • Layover. With the radar looking down at 23° from vertical, the top of a tall building is imaged at the same range as ground far in front of it and its echo may be lost among those.
  • Facade orientation. The upper parts of the towers may simply have no surfaces facing the satellite steadily enough.

The answer decides whether the real ceiling of the method in this city is 83 m or 150 m. It is the first item on the open questions page.

Signs and zeros

Two conventions are used throughout this site and neither is confirmed by the data's producer. First, a positive displacement is towards the satellite; for the vertical part of the motion that means up, so a sinking building has a negative rate. All the plots follow that reading and the figures with signed colours say "blue = away from the satellite". Second, the displacements are zero on 10 January 2014 for every point, so that pass is the reference of the time series; whether it is also the master image of the radar processing is a question for its producer. There is also a reference point, one reflector against which every displacement is measured, and we do not know where it is, how high, or what it stands on. Why that matters is on the buildings and ground page.

The ground truth

Two weather records are used to check the radar against the air.

  • De Bilt soundings (NOAA IGRA2 archive, 4 318 soundings 2014 to 2023). The balloon launched at midnight 50 km away, with its first archived reading typically 650 m above the ground, so its value for our 0 to 83 m layer is an interpolation. Over that layer it says the refractivity falls by 3.4 N-units on average, 2.3 of which is the predictable pressure part and 1.2 the wet part that varies (standard deviation 1.0). Its 11-day change of the layer delay, 1.18 mm per 100 m, is the reference size for what the radar should see.
  • KNMI Rotterdam (station 344, daily mean, minimum and maximum temperature and humidity, CC BY 4.0). The stand-in for the temperature of the buildings, lagged by five days.

The rule: aggregates only

The radar point set was provided for the course and may not be passed on. Nothing on this site is a radar point. The animations and the 3D view are drawn from small tables of averages: 25 m cells, buildings, 10 m slabs of buildings, height bands and the whole scene, each a mean over at least 20 points. The positions that appear are those of public things, building outlines from the national register and cells on a fixed grid. A script in the site repository checks every file against that rule before it is published.
