Paper and team

The whole team writes one manuscript. Its draft is not on this site until it is submitted; this page says what it is about and how it is organised.

The paper

Reconstruction of the near-surface atmospheric refractivity profile from satellite InSAR: potential and limitations. Submission and final presentation: 22 October 2026.

The success criterion is the refractivity of the near-surface air, one number that combines pressure, temperature and humidity. Separating pressure, temperature and humidity is the stretch goal.

How it is organised

ChapterWhat it covers
1 IntroductionWhy cities need repeated profiles of the lowest air; what radiosondes observe today; the urban heat island; weather forecasting
2 TheoryRadar phase and propagation delay; refractivity of moist air and its sensitivity to humidity and temperature; surface delay models; the temperature effect; from an integrated delay to a profile
3 MethodologyThe end-to-end simulation; refractivity and delay; making residuals; temporal double differences; reference choices for the radar time series (draft); separating refractivity from building movement
4 ResultsRadiosonde reference results; joint atmospheric estimation; comparison with GNSS
5 to 7Discussion and future research questions; impact and limitations; conclusions
AppendixBackground on radio-wave propagation
