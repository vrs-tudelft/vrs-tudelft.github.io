A weather balloon that never launches

We are trying to read the lowest few hundred metres of the air above a city from satellite radar, using the buildings themselves as the measuring stick.

A radiosonde is the instrument on a weather balloon. It measures pressure, temperature and humidity all the way up. It is the reference measurement, and it is rare: the Netherlands launches from one place, De Bilt, once a day, and never over a city. Cities are where the air near the ground is most disturbed and where knowing it matters most.

Radar satellites see thousands of fixed points on building facades. A point high on a tower and a point near the street see slightly different amounts of air between them and the satellite. The difference depends on how humid and how warm that layer of air is. If we can separate that small effect from everything else that moves a building, we get a reading of the air between the two heights, every time the satellite passes.

This is a student team project for the course Applied Space Geodesy at TU Delft (September to October 2026), supervised by Prof. Ramon Hanssen. Everything here is work in progress. Nothing on this site is a validated result unless it says so.

What we have so far

Our first dataset covers Kop van Zuid in Rotterdam: 20 594 radar points on buildings, observed 288 times over ten years by the TerraSAR-X satellite. These are the numbers everything else builds on.

NumberValueWhat it means, and the catch
Time between satellite passes11 daysVerified from the 288 dates. So this can never be an hourly or real-time product; it is a long-term record.
Typical height of a radar point14.7 m above groundHalf the points are within 31 m of each other in height. Nothing sits above 83 m, even though there are 150 m towers in the scene. We do not know why yet.
How much buildings move with the seasons0.032 mm per m of heightBuildings expand in summer. That signal is three times larger than the air signal we are after, so it has to be modelled, not ignored.
Can we tell buildings and air apart?yes, at 11-day timescalesA joint fit on temperature and on De Bilt humidity separates the two (VIF 1.12). The air term comes out at about 60 % of the size physics predicts.
Where the ground truth comes fromDe Bilt, 50 km awayThe balloon launches around midnight, and its first reading above ground is at about 650 m. Everything we compare against below 100 m is an interpolation.

Kop van Zuid was chosen because it has high-rise. It is a best case, not a city average.
