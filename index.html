Virtual Radiosonde

A weather balloon that never launches

We are trying to read the lowest hundred metres of the air above a city from satellite radar, using the buildings themselves as the measuring stick.

Status on 5 October 2026. The project runs until 22 October 2026.

The idea

A radiosonde measures pressure, temperature and humidity on its way up through the air. The Netherlands launches them from De Bilt, never over a city, and the archived records say little about the lowest few hundred metres. Cities are where that layer matters most: heat, air quality and weather models all depend on it.

Radar satellites see thousands of fixed points on building facades and measure changes in their distance to about a millimetre. The air slows the radar signal down a little. A point high on a tower looks through less of the lowest air than a point near the street. Compare the two, and compare one pass of the satellite with an earlier one, and what is left is mostly the change in the layer of air between the two heights, plus whatever the buildings themselves did.

That last part is the hard part. Buildings settle, and they grow a little when warm and shrink when cold. A tall building grows more, so its expansion looks like a height-dependent air signal. Most of the current work is about telling the two apart honestly, and about knowing where the zero of the measurement is.

TerraSAR-X persistent scatterers over Kop van Zuid, Rotterdam, descending track, 11-day repeat. For two scatterers at different heights the interferometric double difference (point minus point, pass minus reference pass) cancels everything the two share, including the air above both of them. What remains is the change of the integrated delay d = 10-6 ∫ N ds through the layer between the two heights, plus differential deformation and noise. The target product is the refractivity N of near-surface layers; pressure, temperature and humidity separately are a stretch goal. Every result is relative in space and in time: an absolute profile needs an external reference.

Where the project stands

The work follows one chain, from the inputs to the paper. Each stage has its own page.

  1. Datain hand

    Two radar processings of the same district, building heights, balloon soundings, weather stations and two measurement towers.

  2. Air near the groundfirst version, 1 Oct

    A reconstruction of the lowest 200 m of air from soundings, surface weather and a weather model, checked against two towers.

  3. Simulationbeing rebuilt

    A known atmosphere pushed through the radar geometry and back, to see what can be recovered. The model is being updated.

  4. Referenceadopted, 5 Oct

    Where the zero of the measurement sits. Both processings are tied to the ground points they share, adopted by the team on 5 October.

  5. Estimationpilot, 30 Sep

    Removing what the buildings do. The reference is handled; separating building expansion from temperature-linked air is not solved.

  6. Paperdue 22 Oct

    One manuscript by the whole team. The draft is not on this site.

What holds so far, and what does not yet

There is no observed refractivity or humidity profile on this site yet. What the analyses so far do support:

  • The two radar processings disagree on how fast every point moves by a near-constant amount. Tying both to the ground points they share removes most of it, from +0.60 to +0.11 mm per year. Reference
  • Changing the reference does not change the height contrast the method relies on, to numerical round-off. Estimation
  • The correction for building expansion can also remove the air signal when the air follows temperature. In a synthetic test with correlation 0.9, only 19 % of a known air pattern survives. Estimation
  • Changes over time alone cannot give an absolute profile; one external reference profile is needed. Estimation
  • Between the lowest and highest sensor of the Brasschaat tower (0.1 and 32.8 m), the low-level temperature shape predicts the eight withheld levels better than a straight line between the two: error 0.522 to 0.357 K. Air near the ground

No number on this site is invented: each one comes from a dated note in the team repository.

What is on this site, and what is not

The TerraSAR-X point sets were provided for the course and may not be passed on. No radar point, position or single time series is here. Everything drawn from the radar data is a mean over at least 20 points, shown with the spread of the points behind it, or comes from fully synthetic tests. A script checks every published data file against that rule. The draft text of the paper is not here either. The full rule is on the data page.
