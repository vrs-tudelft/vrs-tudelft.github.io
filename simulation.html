Virtual Radiosonde

Simulation: model update pending

A simulation starts from an atmosphere we choose, works out what the radar would measure, and then tries to get the atmosphere back. Because the answer is known, it shows what the method can and cannot recover.

Status on 5 October 2026: being rebuilt. Nothing on this page describes the final model.

What the simulation is for

The real radar data mix the air with building movement, noise and an unknown reference. A simulation lets the team switch each of those on and off. It answers questions the real data cannot: how many radar points and which spread of heights are needed, and at which noise level an air signal still comes through.

The chain: pressure, temperature and humidity at a set of heights; refractivity; the cumulative delay from the ground up; the delay at each scatterer height; line-of-sight phase; added nuisances (deformation, thermal expansion, noise); double differences; inversion back to refractivity; comparison with the input. The forward part follows the theory section of the paper.

Questions it is meant to answer

  • With the real building heights of Kop van Zuid, how many radar points are needed per height layer?
  • How do noise, building movement and thermal expansion affect the estimated refractivity?
  • Can the building correction tested on the estimation page keep a realistic air signal, and how much of it?
