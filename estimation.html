Estimation: from radar points to an air profile

A higher radar point looks through more near-surface air. The estimation separates the height-dependent atmospheric contribution from building motion, then estimates refractivity in layers on each satellite pass.

The estimation steps

  1. Assign radar points to the 3DBAG buildings and choose shared ground reference points.
  2. Model sinking, height-dependent shrinking and thermal expansion. Use hourly station temperature with an exponential 72 h building-temperature memory.
  3. Fit the remaining height contrast as refractivity in the selected layers. Each layer needs at least 100 points.
  4. Compare the estimated anomalies with simulated truth or, on the real data, the station-based proxy.

Separating buildings and air

Both thermal expansion and the air can track temperature. A building correction can therefore remove part of the atmosphere too. The report's default fits motion per building without an air prior; the per-scatterer fit and station-based air prior are comparisons. The prior carries the station anomaly into the retrieval and must not be treated as independent agreement with that station.

The reference and the missing zero

Radar differences do not give an absolute air profile on the first pass. The shared ground reference sets the zero, and calibration supplies the mean profile. The pass-to-pass anomaly and its height contrast are distinct from that chosen absolute level.

A simulation with known truth

Why simulate

The real scene has no collocated vertical truth. The simulation tests the same estimation against an atmosphere whose refractivity is known.

From atmosphere to radar

The forward model constructs pressure, temperature and vapour, computes refractivity and path delay, samples it at radar points, then adds sinking, shrinking, thermal expansion, cross-range error and constant Gaussian noise before differencing. The experimental amplitude-noise model remains separate.

Frozen samples

The report uses 213 radar-date model epochs, 361 selected sounding epochs and 116 Cabauw mornings. Their original input products are frozen; the newer corrected weather archive is not silently substituted.

Simulation limits

The constructed city and disturbance model are controlled experiments. Success there does not establish vertical accuracy over Rotterdam.

What was reproduced

The frozen real run and twelve report simulation cases were regenerated from committed source. All 867,805 compared numeric values match the published export exactly. This covers both motion models and four layer choices in those cases; it does not claim a rerun of the entire exploratory grid. Preserved optional per-pass vapour arrays are identified separately in the private release manifest.

Start with the ideal case

The retained ideal fixtures test the atmosphere-to-radar and radar-to-profile chain without the full disturbance set. Add disturbances in the simulator to inspect where recovery changes.

Read the real-data comparison

The Results page compares the report variants and plots the same per-pass anomalies. The default is two layers, motion per building and no air prior. Correlation with a ground station is a boundary check, not a measured validation of the upper layer.

Choices that matter

Layer boundaries, point counts, reference selection, the thermal-memory assumption and the motion model affect the recovered profile. Keep those settings with every result. P and T are carried upward from surface observations; radar-derived N does not independently measure all of P, T and vapour.

What remains unresolved

Collocated air profiles on pass dates are still needed to validate vertical accuracy. Building-wide errors and temperature-correlated atmosphere can survive the fit. Station-prior results reuse station information, and a single correlation cannot establish retrieval skill.

Earlier experiments

The October 12-13 summaries used a different point selection, layer top and daily-temperature lag. They are superseded here by the report run. Their code and exports remain recoverable in repository history; they are not a second current result presentation.
