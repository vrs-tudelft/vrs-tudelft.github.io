        What this means

          How it works
          1. 1. Simulate

            A synthetic city under a known atmosphere: built from KNMI weather, from the 2025 balloon soundings, or from the Cabauw tower. The radar distances are worked out pass by pass, with the disturbances you switched on.

          2. 2. Double differences

            Each point is compared with a point lower down, and each pass with a reference pass. What is left is mostly the air between the two heights, plus what the buildings did.

          3. 3. Estimate

            The building movement is fitted and removed, and the remaining delay is turned into refractivity per height layer.

          4. 4. Compare

            The estimate is set against the true layer mean on every pass, and against what a weather station on the ground alone would have said.

          5. 5. Step by step

            The same estimation is run twice more with parts switched off: once removing only each point's own offset (the layer inversion alone), once with the building motion fitted but no thermal term. Panels (a) and (b) show what each part adds, as in Jaap's figure.

          The simulation is described on Simulation, the estimation and its limits on Estimation. A good score here is necessary, not sufficient: the simulated city behaves exactly as the model says it does, the real one does not.

          Simulation (settings of 06_simulation on main, seed 0, rerun through estimation.sim_import). Sinking: use_sinking; shrinking: use_shrinking; thermal dilation: use_thermal; cross-range: use_cross_range, a position error across the beam times the perpendicular baseline; noise: use_noise and noise_mm, Gaussian per point and pass (the real data: about 2.07 mm). One simulation per atmosphere, disturbance set, noise level and point count: 384 in all.

          Estimation (04_python/estimation, defaults: thermal fitted, air prior from the ground, mean boundary; as building temperature the one the simulation used: KNMI 344 daily with the five-day lag for the KNMI-built air, the air's own surface temperature at the pass, no lag, for the soundings and the tower). Each simulation is estimated with four layer setups: 20 m, 40 m, two layers (3.2-43.2 m and above) and one layer.

          Comparison. Radar error: the estimated layer refractivity minus the true layer mean, rms over the passes. Weather station alone: the run's own ground refractivity carried up with −55 N/km. Skill: 1 minus the radar's squared error over the station's; above 0 the radar adds something. Column error: the same for the thickness-weighted mean of all layers. Step by step (Jaap's 05_results/26_long_term_temperature/cabauw_ideal.py): the estimation's N anomaly minus the truth's, with only the point offsets removed (fit_report_group replaced), with thermal="none", and standard; the station alone is the ground N anomaly in every layer; the predicted error is minus the straight line through the truth anomaly minus the ground anomaly (mean of the layers), with its mean removed. The attribution and the cumulative trend read the column error of the other combinations from the manifest. Everything is written by 04_python/estimation/site_simulator.py into data/simulator/; the page only reads those files.
