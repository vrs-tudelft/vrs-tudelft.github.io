                          1. 1. Simulate

                            The end-to-end chain of the paper (section 3.1): pressure, temperature and humidity per metre of height → refractivity N → the delay integrated up to each height → the delay at every radar point's height on every pass → differences between two passes → mapped onto the satellite's line of sight → with the building's own movement and the radar noise added on top. A synthetic city under a known atmosphere: built from KNMI weather, from the 2025 balloon soundings, or from the Cabauw tower. The radar distances are worked out pass by pass, with the disturbances you switched on.

                          2. 2. Double differences

                            Each point is compared with a point lower down, and each pass with a reference pass. What is left is mostly the air between the two heights, plus what the buildings did.

                          3. 3. Estimate

                            The building movement is fitted and removed, per building (sinking and shrinking) or per radar point (its own linear and quadratic term in time), and so is the thermal dilation per building; the remaining delay is turned into refractivity per height layer.

                          4. 4. Compare

                            The estimate is set against the true layer mean on every pass, and against what a weather station on the ground alone would have said.

                          5. 5. Step by step

                            The same estimation is run twice more with parts switched off: once removing only each point's own offset (the layer inversion alone), once with the building motion fitted but no thermal term. Panels (a) and (b) show what each part adds, as in Jaap's figure.

                          6. 6. What the fit takes

                            In the simulation the air alone is known, so the motion fit can be repeated on the air alone (it is linear once the shrinking time is fixed). What each fitted term takes from the air is then exact: the card under Disturbances shows it per layer and pass, and how much of it follows the building temperature or a slow trend.

                          Building temperature. Concrete follows the air slowly. The thermal dilation, in the simulation and in the estimation alike, is driven by the memory of the hourly air temperature at KNMI 344: Tb(t) = ∑l T(t − l) e−l/τ / ∑l e−l/τ, l the age of the hour in hours and τ = 72 h, so an hour three days old weighs 1/e (37 %) of the current one.

                          N, P, T and e. N is the radar's refractivity per layer, as the estimation gives it; P, T and e as section C5 of the estimation notebook: P is carried up from the ground pressure (hydrostatic) and T falls 6.5 K per km from the ground temperature, both from the station alone; e follows from the radar's refractivity with P and T known, N = 77.6 (P − e)/T + 72 e/T + 3.75·105 e/T2. Pick the coldest, median or warmest pass, or press Random pass for a new one each time. Each is compared with the simulation's true N, P, T and e, averaged over each layer, and e with the station alone (the ground refractivity carried up with −55 N/km, converted the same way).

                          Real. Picked as the atmosphere: the same three-step estimation on the 213 real TerraSAR-X passes of 2020-2026, on the full-height export (25 667 points, up to about 150 m NAP; the earlier export stopped near 90 m above the ground), the points grouped into buildings by their 3DBAG outlines (a point counts for a building when it lies strictly inside it), with both motion models and the same four layer setups. There is no measured air above the street, so the truth is the KNMI weather station at Rotterdam The Hague Airport (344): its refractivity at the 06 UTC overpass, carried up with −55 N/km. Because the station is the truth there, the reference is the constant air (predicting no change) instead of the station alone, and there is no predicted drift. Under P, T, e, P and T are KNMI 344's alone, with nothing above the street to check them against; the radar's e is set against KNMI 344's e carried up, next to its own uncertainty per pass (the jackknife, one building left out at a time). The disturbances, the noise and the number of points belong to the simulation; in the real data they are what they are.

                          The simulation is described on Simulation, the estimation and its limits on Estimation. A good score here is necessary, not sufficient: the simulated city behaves exactly as the model says it does, the real one does not.

                          Simulation (settings of 06_simulation, seed 0, run in-process by site_simulator.py). Sinking: use_sinking; shrinking: use_shrinking; thermal dilation: use_thermal, driven by the 72 h memory of the KNMI 344 hourly air temperature at each pass (thermal_memory_h); cross-range: use_cross_range, a position error across the beam times the perpendicular baseline; noise: use_noise and noise_mm, Gaussian per point and pass (the real data: about 2.07 mm). No sideways tilt of a building is simulated. One simulation per atmosphere, disturbance set, noise level and point count: 384 in all.

                          Estimation (04_python/estimation, defaults: thermal fitted, air prior from the ground, mean boundary; as building temperature the one the simulation used, the 72 h memory of KNMI 344 hourly). Motion per building: deformation="report"; per scatterer: deformation="scatterer", each point's own a + bt + ct². Each simulation is estimated with both and four layer setups: two layers (3.2-43.2 m and above), one layer, and layers of at least 500 or 1000 facade points (layer_points, the count-based layers of the building assignment). The air the fit takes: site_absorbed.py.

                          Comparison. Radar error: the estimated layer refractivity minus the true layer mean, rms over the passes. Weather station alone: the run's own ground refractivity carried up with −55 N/km. Skill: 1 minus the radar's squared error over the station's; above 0 the radar adds something. Column error: the same for the thickness-weighted mean of all layers. Step by step (Jaap's 05_results/26_long_term_temperature/cabauw_ideal.py): the estimation's N anomaly minus the truth's, with only the point offsets removed (fit_report_group replaced), with thermal="none", and standard; the station alone is the ground N anomaly in every layer; the predicted error is minus the straight line through the truth anomaly minus the ground anomaly (mean of the layers), with its mean removed. The attribution and the cumulative trend read the column error of the other combinations from the manifest. Everything is written by 04_python/estimation/site_simulator.py into data/simulator/; the page only reads those files.
