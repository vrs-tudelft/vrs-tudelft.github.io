How it works
  1. 1. Simulate

    A synthetic city and a known atmosphere. The radar distances are worked out pass by pass, with the disturbances you switched on.

  2. 2. Double differences

    Each point is compared with a point lower down, and each pass with a reference pass. What is left is mostly the air between the two heights, plus what the buildings did.

  3. 3. Estimate

    The building movement is removed with the chosen method and the remaining delay is turned into refractivity per height layer.

  4. 4. Compare

    The estimate is set against the true layer mean on every pass, and against what a weather station on the ground alone would have said.

The simulation is described on Simulation, the estimation and its limits on Estimation. A good score here is necessary, not sufficient: the simulated city behaves exactly as the model says it does, the real one does not.

Simulation (settings of 06_simulation, rerun through estimation.sim_import). Noise: use_noise and noise_mm, Gaussian per point and pass (the real data: about 2.07 mm). Thermal expansion: use_thermal, αη = 7 × 10-6 /K with a five-day lag. Sinking & shrinking: use_displacement, ground and building subsidence plus shrinkage. Cross-range error: use_cross_range, a position error across the beam times the perpendicular baseline. One simulation per combination of these four: 32 in all.

Estimation (04_python/estimation, estimate(thermal, air_prior, bin_width_m)). “Fit α only” fits the thermal coefficient without an air prior, as in the flowchart; “α + air prior” adds a prior on the air, our default; Jaap de Ruiter's method uses a 72-hour temperature memory for the buildings and a temperature-driven air term. Layer thickness is bin_width_m: 20 m, 40 m, or one layer from 3.2 m to the top. Nine estimations per simulation.

Comparison. Radar error: the calibrated layer refractivity minus the true layer mean (true atmosphere on 5 m levels), rms over the passes. Weather station alone: KNMI ground refractivity carried up with −55 N/km. True spread: the standard deviation of the true layer mean over the passes. α: median of the fitted thermal coefficients. Everything is written by 04_python/estimation/site_simulator.py into data/simulator/; the page only reads those files.
