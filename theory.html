Pressure from the ground

Pressure follows hydrostatic balance: it falls exponentially with a scale height of about 8.4 km. Over the lowest 150 m that is almost a straight line, about 1 hPa per 8 to 9 m, whatever the weather. With the calculator's surface pressure: 1008 hPa at 40 m NAP and 995 hPa at 150 m NAP, which by itself lowers N by about 3.7 per 100 m. So P(z) is fixed from one ground observation at almost no cost; this is also why Saastamoinen's hydrostatic delay is accurate to millimetres.

N is not three independent measurements

Pressure, temperature and vapour all affect refractivity. A single radar-derived N cannot independently identify all three.

P and T profiles use surface observations and prescribed physics; the derived vapour pressure is conditional on those assumptions.

The common-mode limit

A contribution shared by both radar points cancels in the height contrast. The ground reference sets the zero; it does not measure an absolute atmosphere.

A profile measurement over the city is needed to validate the vertical retrieval.
