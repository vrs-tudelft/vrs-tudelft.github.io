What to look for

  • Colour by rate: the towers at the north-east end are deep blue, the south-west tip around Hotel New York almost white. Switch to construction year and the orderings match.
  • Colour per slab and switch on the ceiling plane: every tower goes grey above 83 m although the register's geometry continues to 150 m and more.
  • The satellite view with radar lighting: the bright faces are the ones the radar illuminates; the dark faces have no points, and the tall towers hide the ground behind them.
  • Seasonal swing per slab: each tower's colour deepens upward, about 0.03 mm per metre. Buildings peak around day 210, the open quays around day 45, the cells at the towers' feet hardly at all.
  • Play the slider: most passes are pale over the whole pier; some turn the whole scene red or blue at once, never one building alone, and the strip chart spikes on the same passes.
  • The radar rays show why the layer of air is thin and tilted: the lower ray travels about 80 m further through it. The balloon lights the height bands of one pass in turn.

Every colour is a mean over at least 20 radar points on one building, in one 10 m slab, or in one 25 m ground cell; no individual point is in this view. Building models: 3DBAG (TU Delft), CC BY 4.0.
